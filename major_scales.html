Major Scales

Can you play a one-octave major scale, ascending or descending, from any note chosen at random on the fingerboard? Starting with the first finger? How about the fourth?

Investigating this challenge is one way to become familiar with the fingerboard, and it trains one's ears and fingers as well.

The idea is not to procure a book of scales and fingerings (if such a thing existed), but to investigate the matter one's self, in order to *really* learn it.

By now, we've cleared the open strings, and the three patterns for the A major scale apply to all higher positions.

Ideally, combine this with arpeggios. I would play the first E major scale/arpeggio this way:

-----------------------------------------------------
-----------------------------------------------------
-----------------------------------------------------
--------------1-2-1-----------------2---------------
--------0-2-4-------4-2-0---------2---2-------------
--0-2-4-------------------4-2-0-4-------4--0--------

Follow this approach on the other strings, and descending scales as well, and you will vastly improve your grasp of the guitar fingerboard.

Do the same with minor scales, pentatonic scales, whole-tone scales, octatonic scales....etc.
